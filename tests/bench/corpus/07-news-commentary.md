## Summary

**TL;DR:** A panel of campaign veterans and historians concludes debates rarely decide elections outright, but can matter in close races by crystallizing voter perceptions, especially through 90 minutes of unedited exposure and post-debate spin.

- [overview] ABC News panel with George Will, Donna Brazile, Newt Gingrich, Chris Dodd, Richard Norton Smith and Martha Raddatz debates whether presidential debates change elections, using the 2012 Obama-Romney first debate and past cycles.
- [claim] An ABC News analysis of debates in 10 presidential contests from 1960 to 2008 found only one where a debate appeared to switch the lead by a significant margin: 1980. (named: ABC News analysis)
- [claim] George Will argues debates rarely test aptitudes pertinent to presidential duties, but they are now semi-constitutional events candidates must join; in close elections such as 1960, 1976 and 2000 they can plausibly be the margin. (no source)
- [claim] Will says debates that matter usually catalyze inchoate feelings already present in the electorate and make them articulate and powerful; he calls the 2012 Romney surge too soon to judge as durable. (no source)
- [claim] Donna Brazile says debates matter because millions of viewers see the candidates for the first time and can test campaign claims (no source); she describes her Gore campaign rehearsing to compress months of sound bites into concise answers.
- [claim] Newt Gingrich says Romney's Florida primary debate was aggressive and assertive, and the first 2012 general debate was startling because Romney exceeded expectations while Obama underperformed; the matchup made it extraordinary. (no source)
- [claim] Chris Dodd says primary debates differ from general-election debates: primaries require checking constituency boxes, while general debates become portals for judging character and emotional connection. (no source)
- [claim] Richard Norton Smith says social media transforms debate watching by adding instant commentary and reducing the shared, unmediated experience; he suggests debate audiences have shrunk relative to the population and scripted-seeming debates may carry less weight. (no source)
- [claim] Martha Raddatz says the unedited 90 minutes let viewers see candidates' message discipline and demeanor; at the VP debate she felt Biden was too aggressive for the intimate table and Ryan did not jump in more by strategy. (no source)
- [claim] Will lists debate moments that shaped perceptions: Kennedy-Nixon 1960, Ford's Eastern Europe gaffe 1976, Reagan's "there you go again" 1980, Dukakis on capital punishment 1988, Bush checking his watch 1992 and Gore's sighs 2000. (no source)
- [claim] Dodd warns the 2012 race is not over: six hours of presidential and VP debates were scheduled, three remained, and Obama could come back strongly. (no source)

**Assessment:** The panel mixes former campaign operatives with journalists and a historian, so its strategic judgments are informed but interested; the central "only 1980" claim rests on an ABC News analysis whose method and polling evidence should be checked. The 2012 election context is dated, so treat its predictions as historical.
