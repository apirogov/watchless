## Summary

**TL;DR:** Colin Brook, P.Eng and president of WIKA Instruments, recounts his unplanned path into mechanical engineering, early oilfield work, rise through management, and lessons on culture, reflection, and career advice.

- [claim] Colin Brook says he had no clear career plan: he considered law, then followed his engineer father and math/science aptitude into engineering; he describes himself as an effort-dependent, not standout, student.
- [claim] Poor study habits led him into general sciences first; he learned transfer to engineering required excelling in science courses, not just passing, and was surprised by university's lower care/attention than high school.
- [claim] He says mechanical engineering is normally a four-year degree with a co-op option adding a fifth; at his time admission required physics, calculus (Math 30-1), another science, and a second language, though requirements have changed. (no source)
- [claim] His first professional job was with Schlumberger/Dowell as a frac engineer in Grande Prairie starting summer 1998; he trained about three weeks in Kellyville, Oklahoma, worked 15-days-on/5-days-off, and was laid off after about a year.
- [claim] He returned to Edmonton, joined All Temp Sensors as engineering manager, and stayed through its acquisition by WIKA; after ten years as engineering manager he became president, which he calls a ten-year interview process in which the company identified and nurtured talent.
- [claim] As president, he says his time horizon is three to five years and his daily constant is touching base with the team, showing genuine interest, and removing obstacles; he also handles fires and strategy sessions.
- [claim] During the pandemic, he says business suffered in 2020 and improved in 2021; he focused on preserving culture by reinventing social events virtually rather than canceling them and by watching for employees' home pressures.
- [opinion] He says the best part of the job is the people and that working only for a paycheck is a sad state; he tells new employees at orientation that supporting and caring about each other and families matters.
- [procedure] He asks staff between Christmas and New Year's to spend 15 minutes with a favorite drink reflecting on the year at work and home, being proud of accomplishments, family, friends, and colleagues.
- [opinion] For aspiring engineers, he advises working hard with balance, being ready to study and be academically challenged, parking ego, and investing in relationships with genuine interest.
- [claim] The description lists mechanical engineering requirements (bachelor's; master's/doctorate may be required; provincial licensing), pay of $42/hour and $62,588–$98,252, and a projected national labour shortage for 2019–2028 (description).
