## Summary

**TL;DR:** Borrowing game mechanics is fine only if you understand how they shape player behavior and emotion; use the MDA framework and a clear emotional vision to test whether a mechanic fits.

- [claim] MDA splits games into mechanics (rules, systems, stats), dynamics (player behavior and system consequences), and aesthetics (emotional response); designers change mechanics, which cascade into dynamics and aesthetics (named: MDA paper).
- [claim] Alien: Isolation's original checkpoint saves made death cheap and players careless; manual save points that take time to activate made saving tense and reinforced terror and isolation (named: Gary Napper).
- [claim] Flower cut level-ups, spells, resource management, and time limits because those familiar mechanics clashed with its intended calm and relaxation (named: Jenova Chen).
- [claim] A clear vision—Subnautica's "thrill of the unknown," Resident Evil Village's "struggle to survive," FTL's "commanding a starship"—gives designers a test for whether mechanics fit (named: cited talks/interviews).
- [claim] Presentation also creates aesthetics: Dead Space's first soundtrack made players feel heroic, so EA asked for scarier music; DOOM 2016 aligns speed, health-from-enemies, metal, and violent animation with "push forward combat" (named: Jason Graves; no source for DOOM analysis).
- [claim] Mechanics can conflict and aesthetics are subjective: The Callisto Protocol's limited ammo aims for fear but its insta-kill stealth attack can feel powerful; scoring or time pressure can feel competitive to one player and judged or anxious to another (no source).
- [procedure] To use MDA, ask what behavior a mechanic produces, what feeling that behavior produces, and whether both match the game's vision; if they clash, cut or rework the mechanic (no source).

**Assessment:** Brown has standing as a game-design explainer and this lecture-derived essay cites named designers and studios, but the core MDA-to-vision method is interpretive guidance rather than a validated formula; check the cited talks/interviews and note the examples are current only to 2023.
